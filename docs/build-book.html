Section 01 — open decisions

What is waiting on you

The compute question closed on 1 September. Three remain, and nothing else in the project moves until they are answered — every one is human-only work: a browser, a credit card, or a walk into the yard.

D-10 · plan 01-04 Compute — settled ✓ closed 1 Sep

Raspberry Pi 5, 8GB, board-only from pishop.us — $239.75 all-in. Board $175.00 (SC1112), official 27W PD supply $12.95, Active Cooler $10.95, two microSD cards $29.90, case $10.95. Committed at ffba297.

The $65 memory lever was priced and deliberately not pulled: CD-03 is still open and one of its outcomes puts Grafana on this same board. The second SD card is a required line, not a spare — a corrupted card in late November with no spare is a season-ending event.

What this bought ▸ the hook switch is now free — GPIO header, normally-closed lever-arm microswitch on an internal pull-up, $0.00 of added hardware and no second device in the system. The laptop path needed a Pico H plus a cable plus USB serial firmware. The three HW-04 status LEDs come off the same 40-pin header.

Unblocked 01-07 (can now size the PoE splitter) and 01-08 (has an orderable list)

OPS-03 · plan 01-06 Set the OpenAI spend ceiling and revoke the old key

Roughly 20 minutes at platform.openai.com. Create a call-santa-prod project, set a $150 hard limit with the enforce toggle actually ON — a dollar figure with the toggle off is an alert, not a cap, and that is the usual way this gets marked done while protecting nothing — then a $175 org limit above it, alerts at $75 / $112.50 / $135, and confirm auto-recharge is off.

Then revoke the VITE_OPENAI_API_KEY in voice/my-project/.env. That key was written to be inlined into a browser bundle, so it is compromised by design regardless of where it was deployed. Its revocation is the gate on deleting voice/ — an irreversible step that must not run while a live key exists.

Before you type $150 ▸ at gpt-realtime-2.1 that is ~312 four-minute calls, about 10 calls/day. The mini model buys ~937, about 30/day. At a busy 30/day the full model exhausts the cap around December 10 — and OpenAI monthly limits do not reset until January 1, after the season is over.

Blocks 01-09, 01-10 · answer with configured

CD-06 / CD-07 · plan 01-05 Place the long-lead orders

Reprioritized 1 September. This was the critical path on the premise that the enclosure had the longest lead time in the project. It no longer is — the owner will build custom housing if no stock unit fits (D-28), and the concrete footing is gone entirely (D-27), taking its cure-time urgency with it. Order compute first, then connectivity and power, then the phone. The enclosure and its mount follow whenever they settle.

Buy against the spec qualifiers, not the product titles: enclosure B0CPN4K6LP (~$150–230, must be solid wood or weatherproof composite, interior ≥ 15×13×20", outdoor-rated in the vendor's own words, 4×4-post mountable), rotary phone B00A8BOTWQ (~$70, full body with intact cradle — not a bare handset), plus post, fast-setting concrete, IP68 glands, both sealants, breather vent and desiccant.

Two spec traps on the connectivity order ▸ Cat6 must be solid bare copper, not CCA — copper-clad aluminium's voltage drop across a ~150 ft PoE run browns out the board under load. And PoE must be 802.3at (30W) with a 5V/5A USB-C PD splitter; the Pi 5 needs 27W and 802.3af tops out at 15.4W. Both failures present in December as random mid-call audio dropouts.

Blocks 01-08, 01-10 · answer with ordered

CD-04a · plan 01-03 Ethernet or wireless — the yard visit

Walked on 1 September; the form is still blank. Owner-reported and not yet recorded as evidence: ~150 ft total run, exit through the existing AT&T fiber penetration so no new hole in brick, surface conduit rather than burial which avoids the 811 locate under the form's own rule. That trends FEASIBLE on all three criteria. Still missing: the spot marker and landmark reference, the outlet inspection, the sealing method, and a measured rather than estimated run length.

FEASIBLE requires all three: a measured run ≤ 300 ft, a sealable exit point with a named method, and no trenching under a driveway or 811 locate you won't actually schedule. A run that is technically possible only via a route you don't want to cut is still not feasible — a technical yes that never gets executed sends the build down the wrong branch and takes the bill of materials with it.

What it changes ▸ ethernet lets Phase 1 skip three RF survey windows and swaps the extension cord for direct-burial Cat6 and PoE — one cable carrying both power and network. Wireless keeps the cord and adds the full survey.

Section 02 — architecture

How a voice gets to Santa and back

One unified speech-to-speech loop, not a pipeline of three vendors. That choice is locked (CD-01) and it is why per-stage latency metrics were dropped from the spec — there are no stage boundaries to measure.

The proof of concept is evidence, not a head start

poc/index.html holds the entire control loop in 320 lines of browser JavaScript — the WebRTC handshake, getUserMedia mic capture, every timer, the LED states and the teardown. The target has no browser, no screen and no getUserMedia anywhere in it.

Only two things transfer directly: the token-broker role in server.py, and the SANTA_INSTRUCTIONS persona string. Phase 2 is a headless re-implementation, not a deployment.

Section 03 — the call

What happens between pick-up and hang-up

Fully specified and already working — but it exists only as browser JavaScript that gets thrown away. These timings are read off the running proof of concept, and they are the contract Phase 2 has to reproduce.

Section 04 — the physical build

A sealed box on a post, at child height

The original document assumed a full-size mahogany walk-in booth at $600–800. That unit prices at $2,195, weighs 200 lbs and is rated indoor-only. Dropping it is the single change that makes this budget work.

Section 05 — schedule

Sixteen weeks, thirteen left

Every structural choice in this plan exists to protect the December date. Hardware with shipping lead time is bought in Phase 1 rather than when it's needed, so shipping runs in parallel with software instead of after it.

  1. 1 · Procurement, site survey, repo cleanupdue Aug 253/10 · 7 days late

    Every long-lead purchase placed and every question that could invalidate the build plan answered. All remaining work is the three decisions in Section 01 — and with the enclosure off the critical path, none of them is gated by a shipping clock any more.

  2. 2 · Headless session coredue Oct 66 weeks

    The whole conversation — pick up, greeting, talk, wrap-up, hang up — running end to end on a bench Pi through a real handset, with no browser and no screen anywhere in the loop. The largest phase by far.

  3. 3 · Physical build & hardware integrationdue Oct 273 weeks

    Out of the bench rig and into the real booth — real cradle switch, real LEDs, sealed against weather, ready to sit outside in the cold.

  4. 4 · Safety guardrail & verificationdue Nov 31 week

    "Zero inappropriate content" stops being an aspiration in a concept document and becomes a decided mechanism plus a test run that passed.

  5. 5 · Scheduling, admin control & system statesdue Nov 172 weeks

    Santa's hours change from a phone in seconds, and the box behaves correctly in all three states — inviting when on, resting when off, talking when in use.

  6. 6 · Observability & unattended operationdue Nov 241 week

    The box reports its own health in a form readable from the couch, alerts when something is actually wrong, and recovers from power blips and network drops without anyone walking outside.

  7. 7 · Outdoor install & season readinessdue Dec 1the date

    The booth is standing in the front yard, powered and connected, and a child who has never seen it before walks up and has a complete conversation with nobody helping.

Where the slack actually is

Phase 1 is seven days late, but Phase 1 is not the risk — it is two weeks of calendar holding six weeks of Phase 2 hostage, and Phase 2 is a from-scratch headless rewrite. The four answers in Section 01 are worth more than any amount of planning, because three of them are purchases and configuration that only wait on a person.

Revised 1 September. The enclosure was the one item where lateness could not be recovered by working harder later — but a custom build and a stake-mounted box remove both the shipping clock and the concrete cure window. Cut from v1, deliberately

  • The camera, entirely — no hardware, no mount point, no video stream, no admin screen, no budget line. The source document contradicted itself across four sections; the scope statement won.
  • Physical traffic awareness — depended on the camera, falls with it.
