`## Phone` (D-17, resolving CD-06). A **full corded or rotary phone**, not a standalone handset —
the moulded cradle, the hook plunger with correct travel, and a handset that physically sits in it
are the hard mechanical parts, and buying them assembled keeps them off Phase 3's critical path.
Reference: ECVISION 1960s Style Rotary Retro Phone in red, Amazon ASIN `B00A8BOTWQ`, expected
roughly $70 — research flagged this price as ASSUMED because Amazon blocks automated fetch, so
actual-at-checkout governs. Acceptable equivalents: any full-body corded or rotary reproduction with
an intact cradle and hook mechanism. Add the explicit exclusion: **do not buy a "USB retro handset"** —
several exist and are plug-and-play, but they enumerate as their own USB audio device and have no
cradle, which buys back the exact problem the full phone solves.
Record, as a note carried to Phase 3 rather than as a purchase: the handset connects to the base with
an RJ9/RJ22 4P4C modular plug at telephone levels while a USB audio adapter expects 3.5mm jacks at
computer levels, so the cord will need re-terminating or a breakout. A spare 3.5mm handset was
considered and explicitly deferred by CONTEXT.md; do not add it.

`## Post-mount hardware` (D-02, ordered now so the footing cures before install): one 4x4 pressure-
treated post at the height that puts the cabinet at child height, fast-setting concrete mix sized for
one post hole, one 4x4 post cap, and post-mount hardware or brackets matching the cabinet's mount.

`## Weatherproofing and cable entry` (D-05 plus RESEARCH "Outdoor Electrical"):
- IP68 compression cable gland, PG or M-series, sized to the cable outside diameter, entering the
  enclosure from **underneath or a downward-facing face** — a top or vertical-face entry invites water
  tracking down the cable into the box. Order a multi-pack; these are the one item plausibly not
  stocked locally, roughly $10, 3-5 day ship.
- Exterior **polyurethane** sealant, paintable, for wood joints — polyurethane adheres to wood far
  better than silicone and takes paint; silicone does neither.
- **100% silicone** sealant for the plexiglass pane and exposed weather edges — up to roughly four
  times the service life of polyurethane in direct sun. The professional approach is to fill with
  polyurethane and cap the exposed bead with silicone.
- A breather / pressure-equalisation vent (Gore-style vented plug, roughly $5-10) or at minimum a
  downward-facing louvre with a bug screen, plus silica gel desiccant packs. A sealed wooden box that
  warms from the compute by day and drops to 20F overnight condenses water on the coldest interior
  surface, which is the electronics. Roughly $15 total and trivially forgotten.
- A handset anchor point for inside the cabinet (D-04) — there is no booth hook to hang the handset
  on. A hook, clip or short retention lanyard.
- Zip ties and cable management.

`## Explicitly excluded from this list`, with the reason on each line: camera or any imaging line
(CD-05, D-15); compute, USB audio adapter, microswitch, LEDs (plan 08 owns these); ethernet cable,
PoE injector, LTE dongle (plan 07 owns these, selected by connectivity branch); weatherproof
extension cord (plan 07 owns it — it is removed from the BOM entirely under the ethernet-plus-PoE
branch); spare 3.5mm handset (deferred by CONTEXT.md).

Close with a `## Expected subtotal` line against the D-14 reference math: roughly $200 enclosure plus
post, concrete, cable and sealant, inside the $850-1,050 ceiling once plan 08's compute lines land.
