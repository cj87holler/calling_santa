<objective>
Answer the question that determines everything else about connectivity: can a wired ethernet cable
actually reach the install spot?

Purpose: D-06 makes wired ethernet the preferred path and requires it to be scoped **before** the
wireless survey is run. That ordering exists because the two outcomes produce completely different
downstream work — under FEASIBLE the wireless survey collapses to a feasibility record and the BOM
gains outdoor-rated Cat6 plus PoE while losing the weatherproof extension cord; under NOT FEASIBLE
plan 07 runs the full three-window RF survey from RESEARCH.md. Scoping it first avoids spending a
weekend on RF measurements that a 60-foot cable run would have made irrelevant.

Output: `ETHERNET-SCOPING.md` with a measured length and an assessed exit point, and
`CONNECTIVITY-BRANCH` containing exactly one word that every downstream assertion reads.

This is a physical-world task. The agent cannot walk a cable route or read a wall. The agent's job
is to hand the human a precise field checklist and then encode the answers.
</objective>

  <action>
Create `evidence/site-survey/ETHERNET-SCOPING.md` as the empty field form described above, then
pause for the human to complete the yard visit and supply the values.

Judgement guidance to print inside the form, so the human is not deciding in a vacuum:
mark `VERDICT: FEASIBLE` when the measured run is at or under 300 ft (the Cat6 limit is 328 ft /
100 m, and the last 28 ft are margin), an exit point exists that can be sealed, and no route obstacle
requires trenching under a driveway or a buried-utility locate the owner is unwilling to schedule.
Mark `VERDICT: NOT FEASIBLE` otherwise. If the run is feasible but only via a route the owner does
not want to cut, that is still NOT FEASIBLE — record the reason honestly rather than recording a
technical yes that will not be executed.

Do not record a verdict without the measured length filled in. D-09 requires the decision to cite a
number.
  </action>
  <how-to-verify>
1. Take a tape measure or measuring wheel, a stake or paver, and a phone to the front yard.
2. Choose and physically mark the exact install spot. This is the spot the cabinet post will be set
   in, and every later measurement references it.
3. Walk the cable route from your router or switch to that spot. Measure it. Write the number down.
4. Find the wall, soffit or rim joist where the cable would exit the house. Look at it. Decide
   whether you could seal that penetration — a gland, a cable boot, or exterior sealant.
5. While you are standing there, look at the outdoor outlet you intend to power the box from. Does
   it have a GFCI (a TEST and RESET button, either on the outlet itself or upstream in the panel)?
   Does it have a bubble-style cover that closes with a plug inserted, or only a flip lid that
   closes when empty? Write both down. Remediation is a later phase; recording it now is the point.
6. Fill every blank in `evidence/site-survey/ETHERNET-SCOPING.md`, set the `VERDICT:` line, and
   write the one-line rationale citing the measured length.

| Physical yard -> committed evidence | A recorded measurement is trusted by every later phase; an unmeasured guess written as a number is undetectable downstream |
| House electrical -> outdoor installation | An outdoor receptacle without GFCI protection is a shock hazard at a device children touch |
| House interior -> exterior penetration | An unsealed cable entry admits water into the wall cavity, not just into the enclosure |
