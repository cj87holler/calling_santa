<objective>
Buy the compute and the electronics that plan 04 decided on.

Purpose: This is the second half of HW-01 and the largest cash line in the BOM. It is deliberately a
separate plan from the enclosure procurement because the compute order is gated on a decision while
the enclosure order is gated on nothing — D-13 requires that plan 05 completed without waiting for
this one.

Two failure modes from research govern the spec qualifiers on this order, and both present in
December as symptoms that look like software bugs. A TRRS-only USB audio adapter cannot connect a
telephone handset's separate mic and receiver pairs, and that is discovered in Phase 3 when it is too
late to reorder. An undersized USB-C supply causes the Pi to throttle USB current, which presents as
random mid-call audio-device dropouts inside a sealed cold box — the hardest possible class of bug to
diagnose.

Output: `pi-and-electronics-order.md` with a complete field set per line item.
</objective>

  <action>
Create the pre-populated order record, then pause for the human to buy and fill it in.

Buy from official Raspberry Pi resellers — `pishop.us` or `canakit.com` — not from Amazon
marketplace sellers. Research observed marketplace markups of 25 to 75 percent over official reseller
price on the Pi 5 SKU specifically.

Print these order-time constraints in the file so they are in front of the human at checkout:

**USB audio adapters — the single most reversible mistake on this order.** Buy 2, from two different
vendors or chipsets. Each must have **two separate 3.5mm jacks, one pink mic-in and one green
line-out**. Confirm the jack layout from the product photo before adding to cart. Many
well-reviewed adapters expose a single combined TRRS headset jack, which a telephone handset's
separate mic pair and receiver pair cannot use without a splitter and an added failure point.
Known-good chipsets: C-Media CM108 or CM119, or TI PCM2902 — all driverless via the USB Audio Class
driver, which is what HW-03 requires. At roughly $5 each the spare is free insurance. A caution
carried from the research: some vendors ship different DACs across SKUs sharing a product family
name, so a good review of one SKU does not transfer to another.

**Power supply.** If a board-only Pi was chosen, the official 27W 5V/5A USB-C PD supply is mandatory,
not optional. A generic charger causes USB current-limit throttling whose December symptom is random
mid-call audio-device disappearance.

**microSD cards.** Two, 32GB or larger. A corrupted card in late November with no spare is a
season-ending event and each card is roughly $12.

**Microswitch.** Normally-closed lever-arm type, with jumper wiring. This is the part HW-02 depends
on, and the phone's own cradle mechanism actuates it.

**LEDs.** Enough for the three-state indicator HW-04 specifies, plus current-limiting resistors.

**Sealed inner enclosure (D-03).** An IP65-or-better gasketed ABS or polycarbonate project box with
internal dimensions at least 1 inch larger than the chosen compute on every axis, plus standoffs or a
mounting bracket. The cabinet's plexiglass door protects only when it is closed, and the interior is
open to the weather every time a child opens it, so the electronics live inside their own sealed box
mounted high and to the rear. Nothing critical sits loose on the cabinet floor. Confirm the box's
internal dimensions against the compute footprint recorded in `COMPUTE-DECISION.md` before ordering.

**Do not order a camera, a camera module, a camera mount, or any imaging component.** CD-05 and D-15
lock it out of v1 and its budget line was removed.
**Do not order an audio HAT.** It costs more, occupies the GPIO header needed for the hook switch and
the LEDs, requires device-tree overlay configuration, and is not driverless — HW-03 says driverless
USB audio. A HAT is a Phase 3 fallback only if USB audio quality proves unacceptable through a
telephone handset.
**Do not order a spare 3.5mm handset.** CONTEXT.md defers it explicitly.

<threat_model>
## Trust Boundaries

| Boundary | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| Third-party marketplace listing -> purchased hardware | Marketplace sellers on the Pi 5 SKU were observed 25-75% above official reseller price with no authenticity guarantee |
| Vendor checkout page -> committed evidence file | Payment material pasted next to an order number lands in a public GitHub repository |
| Product photo -> jack layout assumption | "USB audio adapter with mic support" describes both the working dual-jack layout and the unusable TRRS one |
| Power supply rating -> USB peripheral stability | A brownout presents as intermittent audio-device loss, not as an obvious power fault |

## STRIDE Threat Register

| Threat ID | Category | Component | Disposition | Mitigation Plan |
|-----------|----------|-----------|-------------|-----------------|
| T-1-14 | Tampering | Counterfeit or heavily marked-up Pi board from a marketplace seller | mitigate | Purchasing is restricted to `pishop.us` and `canakit.com`, both official Raspberry Pi resellers, with the observed markup range stated so the constraint is understood rather than obeyed blindly |
| T-1-15 | Denial of Service | Undersized USB-C supply causing USB current-limit throttling | mitigate | The official 27W 5V/5A supply is a mandatory line on any board-only Pi, asserted by an acceptance criterion, with the December symptom named so it is not dropped as an optional extra |
| T-1-16 | Denial of Service | Single point of failure on the USB audio adapter or the microSD card | mitigate | Two adapters from different vendors or chipsets and two microSD cards are mandatory; the acceptance criterion asserts exactly two adapter blocks confirm the dual-jack layout |
| T-1-31 | Denial of Service | A TRRS-only adapter making the handset audio path unbuildable, discovered in Phase 3 | mitigate | `Jack layout confirmed from product photo:` is a required field with only two permitted values, and an acceptance criterion asserts zero blocks confirm `single combined TRRS`. Research pitfall 7 |
| T-1-18 | Information Disclosure | Payment material committed to a public repository | mitigate | Task 2 scans for 13-to-16-digit runs and the strings `CVV` and `password`, removes any hit in place, and flags it as an incident |
| T-1-32 | Denial of Service (scope) | A camera or audio HAT reintroduced through a bundled kit | mitigate | Both are explicit pre-checkout exclusions with the reasons stated (CD-05 and D-15 for the camera; GPIO occupancy and the driverless requirement of HW-03 for the HAT), and acceptance criteria assert zero occurrences |
| T-1-05 | Tampering | Supply chain | accept | No npm, PyPI or crates.io package is installed. Hardware supply chain is addressed by T-1-14 |
</threat_model>
