<objective>
Buy the compute and the electronics that plan 04 decided on.

Purpose: This is the second half of HW-01 and the largest cash line in the BOM. It is deliberately a
separate plan from the enclosure procurement because the compute order is gated on a decision while
the enclosure order is gated on nothing — D-13 requires that plan 05 completed without waiting for
this one.

Two failure modes from research govern the spec qualifiers on this order, and both present in
December as symptoms that look like software bugs. A TRRS-only USB audio adapter cannot connect a
telephone handset's separate mic and receiver pairs, and that is discovered in Phase 3 when it is too
late to reorder. An undersized USB-C supply causes the Pi to throttle USB current, which presents as
random mid-call audio-device dropouts inside a sealed cold box — the hardest possible class of bug to
diagnose.

This plan also carries the phase's largest cash line, so it is where the budget ceiling is checked
while the money is still unspent. Plan 10 reconciles honestly but by then every purchase is
irreversible.

Output: `pi-and-electronics-order.md` with a complete field set per line item.
</objective>

  <action>
State the active entry condition, create the pre-populated order record, then pause for the human to
buy and fill it in.

Buy from official Raspberry Pi resellers — `pishop.us` or `canakit.com` — not from Amazon
marketplace sellers. Research observed marketplace markups of 25 to 75 percent over official reseller
price on the Pi 5 SKU specifically.

**Compute the budget headroom before the human opens a checkout page, and print it in the file** as a
section headed `## Budget headroom at checkout`. Sum every `Actual price paid (USD):` value found in
`enclosure-order.md`, `phone-order.md` and `site-hardware-order.md`. State that sum, state the D-14
ceiling of $1,050, and state `Remaining headroom: $NNN` as the difference. Add the connectivity order
as a pending claim on that headroom if `connectivity-order.md` is not yet filled — plan 07 owns it and
it lands in the same wave. Then state the rule plainly: if the cart total exceeds the remaining
headroom, stop and escalate the overrun with the responsible line named, rather than checking out and
letting plan 10 discover it in wave 5 when every purchase is irreversible.

Print these order-time constraints in the file so they are in front of the human at checkout.

**USB audio adapters — the single most reversible mistake on this order.** Buy 2, from two different
vendors or chipsets. Each must have **two separate 3.5mm jacks, one pink mic-in and one green
line-out**. Confirm the jack layout from the product photo before adding to cart. Many
well-reviewed adapters expose a single combined TRRS headset jack, which a telephone handset's
separate mic pair and receiver pair cannot use without a splitter and an added failure point.
Known-good chipsets: C-Media CM108 or CM119, or TI PCM2902 — all driverless via the USB Audio Class
driver, which is what HW-03 requires. At roughly $5 each the spare is free insurance. A caution
carried from the research: some vendors ship different DACs across SKUs sharing a product family
name, so a good review of one SKU does not transfer to another.

**Power supply.** If a board-only Pi was chosen, the official 27W 5V/5A USB-C PD supply is mandatory,
not optional. A generic charger causes USB current-limit throttling whose December symptom is random
mid-call audio-device disappearance.

**microSD cards.** Two, 32GB or larger, if a Pi was chosen. A corrupted card in late November with no
spare is a season-ending event and each card is roughly $12.

**Microswitch.** Normally-closed lever-arm type, with jumper wiring. This is the part HW-02 depends
on, and the phone's own cradle mechanism actuates it.

**LEDs.** Enough for the three-state indicator HW-04 specifies, plus current-limiting resistors.

**Sealed inner enclosure (D-03).** An IP65-or-better gasketed ABS or polycarbonate project box with
internal dimensions at least 1 inch larger than the chosen compute on every axis, plus standoffs or a
mounting bracket. The cabinet's plexiglass door protects only when it is closed, and the interior is
open to the weather every time a child opens it, so the electronics live inside their own sealed box
mounted high and to the rear. Nothing critical sits loose on the cabinet floor. Confirm the box's
internal dimensions against the compute footprint recorded in `COMPUTE-DECISION.md` before ordering.

**Exclusions.** Three things must not appear on this order, and the wording written into the file
matters because two acceptance criteria grep this file for exactly those words. Write them like this,
and do not paraphrase back toward the forbidden terms:

- **No video or imaging component of any kind** (CD-05, D-15) — no sensor module, no lens, no mount.
  It is locked out of v1 and its budget line was removed.
- **No GPIO audio expansion board** (HW-03 requires a driverless USB audio adapter). Such a board
  costs more, occupies the GPIO header needed for the hook switch and the status LEDs, requires
  device-tree overlay configuration, and is not driverless. It is a Phase 3 fallback only if USB
  audio quality proves unacceptable through a telephone handset.
- **No spare 3.5mm handset.** CONTEXT.md defers it explicitly.

Do not write the word for a photographic device into this file, and do not write the three-letter
acronym for a GPIO expansion board into it either — the exclusions above say the same thing in words
that do not collide with the assertions. A file that states its own prohibition in the prohibited word
fails its own check.
